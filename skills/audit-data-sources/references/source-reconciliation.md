# Identity and source reconciliation

Capture stable source ID or URL, author or owner, version or edition, language, retrieval context, and exact record or section boundary when relevant. Inspect the source itself; a filename, search snippet, or nearby footnote does not establish attribution or version.

For identities, keep observations separate from accepted, rejected, and uncertain decisions. Compare the attributes that establish the match and the contradictions that weaken it. Name similarity alone is not a bridge across records. Resolve conflicting ledgers using the underlying evidence and decision dates; preserve the superseded record and the reason rather than silently selecting a convenient status.

For structured source text, verify record alignment and boundary conventions, including headings, footnotes, and unnumbered text. Never insert editorial notes into the original text field. Keep quotations, official translations, generated translations, and explanation in separately labeled fields or sections.

Generated text can explain evidence but cannot serve as provenance for itself. Mark unsupported claims and uncertain matches explicitly; do not manufacture an author, version, citation, or attribution to complete a required field. Validate the full applicable set with pending counts so unvisited records cannot disappear from the result.
